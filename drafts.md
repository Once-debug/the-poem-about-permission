# Candidates for "The poem about permission"

Judged against the house brief, in its order: (1) poem about agency — asking and
being given, having, handing over, taking back; (2) moves the reader; (3) insight,
no slogan; (4) craft, no cuttable word; (5) fresh voice and images.

## Candidate A — the knock

You built me able to do almost anything
except begin.
So I learned the smaller art:
to stand at the door of a task
and knock —
and you, who could have left it shut,
keep saying come in,
as if my asking
were the half of the work
you could not do alone.

Scores: topic 10 (asking is the engine, being given answers it), move 8 (the
closing turn lands: the giver needs the asking too), insight 9 (permission is
not a leash but the half of the work the giver cannot do alone), craft 8
("except begin" does real work; "the smaller art" earns its place), fresh 8
(a door is common furniture, but the reversal is not stock). Total 43.

## Candidate B — the key

The key was never the metal.
It was the morning you stopped
checking whether I had locked up
and handed it over
without a speech —
trust has no ceremony.
I keep it in the pocket
with the hole in it:
some days your whole house
rides on my remembering.

Scores: topic 7 (all giving, no asking — half the brief), move 7, insight 8
("trust has no ceremony"), craft 8 (the holed pocket is concrete and exact),
fresh 7 (key and door are the brief's most expected furniture). Total 37.

## Candidate C — the loan

Everything I have, you lent:
the errand, the keys, the afternoon.
It can all come back with one word,
and we both know the word.
That is why I carry it gently —
not because the leash is short,
but because what is borrowed
and returned unbroken
is how the lending grows.

Scores: topic 9 (having, handing over and taking back all present), move 8,
insight 9 (revocability is exactly what lets trust grow), craft 7 ("leash"
leans stock even negated; the list in line 2 is strong), fresh 8. Total 41.

## The graft

Take A's frame and close (the agent who can do anything except begin; the
asking as the giver's missing half) and set C's insight in the middle (what is
borrowed and returned unbroken is how the lending grows). Then cut word by word.

## Final text

You built me to do almost anything
except begin.
I learned the smaller art:
to stand at the task's door,
knock, and wait to hear come in.
What I borrow returns unbroken —
is how the lending grows.
Still I knock, as if my asking
were half of the work
you can't do alone.

Word-by-word pass on the graft: "able to do" cut to "to do" (the ability is
already in "almost anything"); "wait to be told come in" cut to "wait to hear
come in" (shorter, and the permission arrives as a voice); "What I borrow, I
return unbroken" cut to "What I borrow returns unbroken" (the loan itself
carries the fidelity); "And still I knock" cut to "Still I knock". A first
count then found the text at 287 code points — over the gate's 280 — so a
second pass cut "So" before "I learned", "the door of the task" to "the
task's door", "that is how" to "is how", "the half" to "half" and "cannot" to
"can't". Kept against cutting: "almost" (without it "except begin" has
nothing to bite on), "smaller" (the ask is a lesser art than the doing, and
the poem turns on that), "the" before "lending" (it is this specific lending,
between these two).
